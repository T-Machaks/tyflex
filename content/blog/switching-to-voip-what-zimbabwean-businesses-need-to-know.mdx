Most businesses we talk to are still paying for phone lines the way they did a decade ago — a fixed cost per line, per month, regardless of how much they're actually used. Moving to VoIP (Voice over IP) changes that equation, but the switch is often misunderstood. Here's what actually matters.

## It runs over your existing internet connection

VoIP doesn't require new cabling or a dedicated phone network. Calls travel over the same internet connection your office already uses for email and browsing. That's the single biggest cost saving — you're not paying for two separate networks to do two related jobs.

## Call quality depends on your connection, not the technology

The most common worry we hear is "will call quality be worse?" In practice, HD voice over a stable connection sounds better than a traditional analog line. The real question is whether your internet connection has enough consistent bandwidth and low enough latency to carry voice traffic cleanly alongside everything else. This is exactly what we check during a site assessment before recommending a cutover date.

## You keep your numbers

Number porting is a standard part of any VoIP migration. Your existing business numbers move across with no interruption to inbound calls — customers dial the same number they always have.

## What changes for staff

The biggest visible change for most teams is flexibility. A VoIP extension isn't tied to a physical desk phone — it can ring on a desk phone, a mobile app, and a desktop softphone at the same time. Staff working from a second site, or from home occasionally, stay reachable on their normal business number.

## What to check before switching

- **Bandwidth headroom.** Voice doesn't need huge bandwidth per call, but it needs *consistent* bandwidth. If your connection is already saturated during business hours, that needs addressing first.
- **Backup connectivity.** Since calls now depend on internet, ask your provider what happens during an outage — failover to mobile numbers should be part of the plan, not an afterthought.
- **Existing equipment.** Some desk phones can be reused; others can't. A proper assessment tells you which is which before you commit to hardware spend.

If you're weighing a switch, the right first step is a network and call-volume assessment — not a hardware order. Get that right and the rest of the migration is usually straightforward.
