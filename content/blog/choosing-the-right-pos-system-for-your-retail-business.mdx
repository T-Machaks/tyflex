Picking a POS system is easy to get wrong in a very specific way: buying hardware that's a great fit for someone else's shop. The right system depends heavily on how you actually sell, not just what's popular.

## Start with your transaction pattern, not the hardware

A single-till boutique with a handful of daily sales has very different needs from a fast-moving retail counter handling a sale every couple of minutes. High-throughput counters benefit most from a fast barcode scanner and a reliable receipt printer with an auto-cutter — the terminal itself matters less than the speed of the whole checkout flow.

## Offline mode is not optional

Connectivity isn't always guaranteed. A POS system that stops taking sales the moment the internet drops is a real business risk, not a minor inconvenience. Look for offline mode that queues transactions locally and syncs automatically once connectivity returns — this should be a baseline requirement, not a premium feature.

## Inventory sync saves more time than it seems

If stock counts are still reconciled manually at the end of the day, a POS system with real-time inventory sync pays for itself quickly. Every sale updates stock automatically, and if you run multiple locations, that visibility extends across branches instead of living in separate spreadsheets.

## Match the cash drawer to your till discipline

A POS-triggered cash drawer sounds like a minor detail until a mismatched till becomes a daily argument. Make sure the drawer's trigger mechanism (usually RJ11 from the receipt printer) is compatible with the terminal and printer you're pairing it with — this is one of the most common mismatch issues we see in DIY setups.

## Plan for growth from day one

Adding a second till or a second location later is far easier if the first system was set up with centralized reporting in mind. Retrofitting that after the fact usually means a full re-platform rather than a simple add-on.

The most reliable way to avoid buying the wrong setup is to walk through a typical hour of trading with whoever's actually going to be behind the counter — the gaps in a spec sheet tend to show up fast once you do.
