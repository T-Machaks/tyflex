Bulk SMS and WhatsApp Business messaging both let you reach customers directly on their phones, and it's tempting to treat them as interchangeable. In practice, they're suited to different jobs — and most businesses end up using both, deliberately.

## Reach is SMS's biggest advantage

Every phone that can receive a call can receive an SMS. There's no app required, no data connection needed at the moment of delivery, and no dependency on the recipient having WhatsApp installed or active. For time-sensitive, universal-reach messages — payment confirmations, appointment reminders, service alerts — SMS is still the most reliable channel available.

## WhatsApp wins on engagement and richness

WhatsApp Business messaging supports images, buttons, quick replies, and threaded conversation — none of which SMS can do. Open and response rates on WhatsApp are typically higher than SMS, and it feels less like a broadcast and more like a conversation. For promotions, product catalogs, and customer support, that richer format usually converts better.

## The approval process is different

SMS campaigns can go out as soon as you have a compliant contact list. WhatsApp Business API messaging requires template approval before you can send certain message types — this isn't a big obstacle, but it does mean WhatsApp campaigns need to be planned a little further ahead than a same-day SMS blast.

## A practical way to split the two

A pattern we see work well for retail and service businesses:

- **SMS** for anything transactional or time-critical — order confirmations, appointment reminders, delivery notifications.
- **WhatsApp** for anything that benefits from richer content or a two-way conversation — promotions, product images, customer support threads.

## Delivery tracking matters either way

Whichever channel you use, insist on visibility into delivery status. A campaign that "sent" but wasn't actually delivered to a meaningful share of your list isn't doing its job — a proper dashboard should show delivery, not just dispatch.

Most businesses don't need to choose one over the other permanently — they need a platform that makes it easy to use the right channel for each message, without switching tools.
